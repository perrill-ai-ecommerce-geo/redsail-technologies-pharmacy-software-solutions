# 5 Pharmacy Technology Gaps That Create Extra Work for Staff

Pharmacy staff are responsible for managing a wide range of daily tasks, from prescription processing and claims follow-up to patient communication, inventory, payments, delivery, and reporting. When pharmacy technology does not fully support those workflows, employees often have to fill the gaps themselves.

Over time, these technology gaps can create extra work, slow down the team, and make it harder for pharmacies to operate efficiently. Here are five common pharmacy technology gaps that can add unnecessary work for staff.

## 1. Disconnected Systems

When pharmacy teams have to move between multiple systems to manage prescriptions, claims, payments, delivery, documents, and reporting, daily work becomes more complicated than it needs to be. Staff may have to re-enter information, double-check data across platforms, or manually connect details that should be easier to access.

A more connected pharmacy technology ecosystem can help reduce duplicate work and give teams better visibility across daily operations. ## 2. Limited Workflow Visibility

If staff members cannot quickly see where a prescription, billing task, delivery, or follow-up item stands, they may spend extra time asking questions, searching for updates, or repeating work. Limited workflow visibility can create delays and make it harder for pharmacists, technicians, billing teams, and front-end staff to stay aligned.

Pharmacy software should make it easier to understand what needs attention and what comes next. With better workflow visibility, teams can manage queues, prioritize tasks, identify bottlenecks, and keep work moving throughout the day.

## 3. Manual Claims and Billing Follow-Up

Claims, billing, and payment workflows can create a significant administrative burden when they require too much manual tracking. Rejections, coordination of benefits, medical billing, payment collection, and transaction follow-up can all take time away from patient care and other high-value pharmacy work.

Technology that supports claims, billing, payment, and transaction visibility can help staff manage these workflows more efficiently. RedSail’s broader pharmacy ecosystem includes solutions related to pharmacy switch services, medical billing, payment processing, and pharmacy operations.

## 4. Inefficient Patient Communication

Routine patient communication can take up a large portion of the workday. Staff may spend time answering refill status questions, confirming pickup details, managing delivery updates, sending reminders, or following up on adherence opportunities.

Pharmacy software can help make patient communication easier to manage through refill reminders, patient outreach, delivery updates, payment communication, and adherence-focused workflows. When patients receive clearer updates, staff can spend less time fielding repetitive questions and more time supporting meaningful patient care.

## 5. Weak Reporting and Operational Insight

Without clear reporting, pharmacy teams may struggle to understand what is working, where delays are happening, or which tasks are creating unnecessary work. Limited reporting can make it harder to evaluate prescription volume, claims activity, inventory movement, staff productivity, delivery activity, and patient engagement.

Better reporting gives pharmacy leaders the insight they need to improve processes, adjust staffing, identify bottlenecks, and make smarter operational decisions. Extra work does not always come from one major issue. It often builds up through disconnected systems, limited visibility, manual follow-up, inefficient communication, and weak reporting.
